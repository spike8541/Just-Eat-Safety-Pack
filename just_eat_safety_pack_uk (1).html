Before You Start Delivering

Video not loading? Watch “Before you start delivering” on YouTube. The rest of this section covers the same material in text.

Welcome to Just Eat safety — your safety and well-being is paramount. As a Just Eat courier, you play a vital role in connecting customers with their favourite food, and we want to ensure you stay safe while doing so.

Your Obligations

  • Understand the hazards you may face while delivering
  • Take reasonable steps to ensure your health and safety and that of others
  • Comply with health and safety programs put in place by Just Eat
  • Report all incidents to Just Eat through the appropriate channels
  • In emergencies, always call 999 immediately

Vehicle Compliance

Bicycles and E-bikes:

  • Must have two independent braking systems
  • Working bell is required at all times
  • Vehicle must be maintained in a roadworthy state
  • During hours of darkness or poor visibility: front white/yellow light + rear red light + red rear reflector required
  • Reflectors on pedals or wheel spokes required

E-bikes specifically:

  • Maximum 250W continuous rated power
  • Motor assistance must cut off at 25 km/h
  • Pedal assist only — motor stops when pedaling stops
  • Must display CE marking

Pre-Start Equipment Safety Check (60-Second Check)

Before every delivery run, perform this quick check:

  • Tyres: Firm and free of cuts or visible damage
  • Brakes: Test both front and rear brakes
  • Chain: Oiled and gears shift smoothly
  • Lights: Working and reflectors clean
  • Battery: Fully charged and locked in place (e-bike)
  • Handlebars/Seat/Pedals: Tightly secured

Take a short test ride before accepting your first order to ensure everything is working properly.

Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

  • Helmet: Must meet standard EN 1078. Ensure it's fastened and fits comfortably. Replace every 3-5 years or after any impact.
  • High-visibility clothing: Wear outer-wear meeting ISO 20471 standards
  • Footwear: Non-slip, covered shoes. Avoid open-toe shoes.
  • Eye protection: Shatterproof safety-style sunglasses, clean and scratch-free

Vehicle Compliance

Cars:

  • Vehicle must be roadworthy and maintained in safe condition
  • Valid registration and MOT (if applicable)
  • Appropriate insurance including Hire and Reward cover
  • Road tax must be current
  • All lights, indicators, and wipers must be functioning

Pre-Drive Vehicle Check

  • Tyres: Correct pressure, sufficient tread (minimum 1.6mm), no cuts or bulges
  • Brakes: Test brake pedal feel, check handbrake holds
  • Fluids: Oil, coolant, screenwash at correct levels
  • Lights: Headlights, indicators, brake lights all working
  • Mirrors: Clean and correctly adjusted
  • Seatbelt: Functioning and worn at all times

Personal Protective Equipment

  • Seatbelt must be worn at all times — it's the law
  • Comfortable, non-slip footwear for driving and walking to doors
  • High-visibility vest for stepping out of the vehicle at night or in poor visibility
  • Keep a phone mount for navigation — never hold phone while driving

Vehicle Compliance

Motorcycles and Scooters:

  • Valid license appropriate for vehicle type (CBT, A1, A2, A, AM)
  • Vehicle must be correctly registered
  • Appropriate insurance including Hire and Reward cover
  • Road tax must be current
  • MOT required for vehicles over 3 years old
  • All lights, indicators, and horn must be functioning
  • No illegal modifications to engine or exhaust

Pre-Ride Vehicle Check

  • Tyres: Correct pressure, sufficient tread, no cuts or cracks
  • Brakes: Test both front and rear brakes, check brake fluid
  • Chain: Correctly tensioned and lubricated
  • Lights: Headlight, brake light, indicators all working
  • Mirrors: Clean and correctly adjusted
  • Fuel/Battery: Sufficient for the delivery run

Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

  • Helmet: Must meet BS 6658:1985 (with BSI Kitemark) or UNECE Regulation 22.05 — legally required
  • Helmet must fit securely and visor must be clean and scratch-free
  • If no visor: wear wraparound sunglasses with UV protection
  • High-visibility clothing with reflective elements
  • Gloves: Protect hands from debris and in case of fall
  • Footwear: Sturdy closed-toe shoes or boots, flat-soled
  • Weather-appropriate layers: moisture-wicking base, insulation, windproof/waterproof outer

Prepare Your Smartphone

  • Mount it securely on your vehicle
  • Set up navigation before riding — avoid fiddling with your phone while on the move
  • Avoid distractions while riding

Get Your Thermal Bag Ready

  • Use a commercial grade thermal bag
  • Clean the inside before each delivery run
  • Load heavy items first and lighter items last
  • Don't overfill the bag
  • Affix straps tightly to secure the bag
✔ Safety Tip

Always perform your safety check before every delivery run — it could prevent an accident.

Road Safety

Video not loading? Watch “Road safety for couriers” on YouTube. The rest of this section covers the same material in text.

Staying safe on the roads is essential. Always be alert and aware of your surroundings. Your safety depends on anticipating hazards and reacting appropriately.

Visibility

  • Wear bright reflective clothing, especially during low-light conditions
  • Ensure functioning front and rear lights at all times
  • Use lights during dusk, dawn, and poor weather conditions

Intersections

  • Obey all traffic signals and road markings
  • Make eye contact with other road users before proceeding
  • Signal clearly to indicate your intentions

Weather Conditions

Reduce your speed in rain, snow, or ice. Increase your following distance from vehicles ahead. Be extra cautious on wet surfaces where braking distances are longer.

Lane Selection

  • Use designated cycle lanes when available — stay within the lines
  • On regular roads, position about one meter from the right edge to be visible to motorists
  • Avoid hugging the curb as this can force dangerous overtaking by vehicles
  • Be aware of traffic and adjust your position at intersections and turns

Road Positioning

Improve your visibility through smart road positioning. Maintain a safe distance from curbs and parked cars to avoid the "door zone" — the area where car doors may suddenly open.

Road Hazards

  • Potholes, cracks, uneven pavement: Scan ahead, reduce speed, use robust tyres
  • Slippery surfaces (wet roads, tram tracks): Cross tracks perpendicularly, reduce speed, avoid sudden manoeuvres
  • Opening car doors: Maintain safe distance from parked cars, anticipate openings, use your bell to alert drivers

Safe Driving Practices

  • Always wear your seatbelt
  • Obey speed limits — they're there for your safety
  • Never use your phone while driving — pull over safely first
  • Maintain a safe following distance (2-second rule in dry, 4-second in wet)
  • Use indicators early to signal your intentions

Parking Safely

  • Park in well-lit, designated areas when collecting or delivering
  • Check mirrors and blind spots before opening doors
  • Apply handbrake and switch off engine when stationary
  • Reverse into parking spaces where possible for easier exit

Junctions and Roundabouts

  • Approach at appropriate speed
  • Check all mirrors before manoeuvring
  • Watch for cyclists and motorcyclists, especially in blind spots
  • Give way to pedestrians at crossings

Road Positioning and Buffering

  • Position yourself to maximise distance from hazards (buffering)
  • Stay visible — avoid riding in other vehicles' blind spots
  • Position to the left or right of your lane to be seen more easily
  • Be aware of oil spills, especially at junctions and in wet weather

Lane Filtering

  • Filter slowly and carefully in slow-moving or stationary traffic
  • Never filter at high speeds
  • Watch for vehicles changing lanes suddenly
  • Be extra cautious near junctions — drivers may pull out without seeing you

Cornering

  • Slow down before entering a corner, accelerate gently through it
  • Look where you want to go — your bike follows your eyes
  • Avoid sudden braking mid-corner

Road Hazards

  • Potholes and debris: Scan ahead, steer around smoothly
  • Wet road markings and manhole covers: Extremely slippery when wet
  • Diesel spills: Rainbow-coloured patches on the road, avoid them
  • Opening car doors: Maintain distance from parked cars
⚠ Warning

Never ride or drive under the influence of drugs, alcohol, or medication that may impair your ability to ride safely.

At the Partner (Restaurant Collection)

Video not loading? Watch “Collecting an order at the restaurant” on YouTube. The rest of this section covers the same material in text.

Safe collection at the restaurant partner is essential for a smooth delivery experience. Follow these guidelines to ensure professional and safe interactions.

Before Arrival

  • Check the app for special accessibility instructions
  • Note designated courier entrances, parking locations, and waiting areas

Upon Arrival

  • Park your vehicle safely and securely
  • Update your status in the app ("Parked at Restaurant" or "I've Arrived")

Entering the Restaurant

  • Walk in carrying your insulated bag to identify yourself as a courier
  • Interact politely and professionally with restaurant staff

Order Handling

  • If the order isn't ready, wait in the vicinity of the restaurant to ensure food stays at the right temperature
  • Place items into your thermal/insulated bag carefully
  • Separate hot and cold items to maintain food quality

Age-Restricted Items

Follow the Challenge 25 policy for age-restricted items:

  • Verify the customer's ID has a photo, date of birth, and holographic/UV feature
  • Do not hand over if the person appears underage or intoxicated

After Collecting

  • Confirm collection in the app
  • Depart promptly
  • Wear appropriate footwear: closed-toe, flat shoes with good grip to navigate potentially slippery surfaces
✔ Safety Tip

Always carry your thermal bag into the restaurant — it identifies you as a courier and keeps food at the right temperature.

At the Customer (Delivery)

Video not loading? Watch “Delivering safely to the customer” on YouTube. The rest of this section covers the same material in text.

Safe delivery at the customer's door is the final step in completing a successful delivery. Your personal safety is always the top priority.

General Safety Rules

  • Never enter a customer's private property or residence
  • Prioritise personal safety above all — if a situation feels unsafe, you have the right to leave immediately
  • Be aware of your surroundings, especially during late-night deliveries or in unfamiliar areas
  • Stick to well-lit and populated areas whenever possible

Multi-Unit Buildings (Flats)

  • Bring orders to the door unless the customer has specified meeting elsewhere
  • Only enter the building if you feel safe doing so

Professional Interaction

  • Maintain polite communication even when faced with frustration
  • Practice active listening
  • Remain calm under pressure
  • Use non-verbal communication: maintain open body language and keep a safe distance

Dealing with Difficult Situations

  • If faced with verbal abuse, threats of violence, or encounters with intoxicated/unstable individuals: immediately disengage and prioritise your safety
  • For urgent safety threats: contact emergency services (999) directly
  • Report all incidents or harassment via the courier app's support chat

Age-Restricted Deliveries

Follow the Challenge 25 policy:

  • Verify ID (passport, driver's license) with photo, date of birth, and holographic/UV feature
  • Do not hand over if the person appears intoxicated or cannot provide valid ID

Property Respect

  • Use designated walkways
  • Avoid lawns or gardens
  • Ensure gates are properly latched after passing through
  • Keep emergency contact information accessible on your person

Parking at the Customer

  • Park legally and safely — never block driveways or park on yellow lines
  • Turn off engine and remove keys when leaving the vehicle
  • Lock all doors — do not leave delivery bags visible in the car
  • Check surroundings before opening your door

Parking at the Customer

  • Park on firm, level ground — avoid soft verges or steep cambers
  • Use your steering lock and secure the bike
  • Do not leave your helmet on the bike — take it with you
  • Be mindful of where you place your delivery bag — keep it off the ground
⚠ Warning

If you ever feel unsafe during a delivery, leave immediately and contact support. Your safety is more important than any delivery.

Fatigue and Staying Alert

Fatigue is one of the least visible risks you face. It builds slowly, and by the time you notice it your reactions have already slowed. Research suggests tiredness contributes to up to 20% of collisions on major UK roads, and fatigue-related crashes are more likely to be serious because a tired rider or driver often doesn't brake or swerve at all.

What the Highway Code says

Rule 91 sets out the baseline for anyone on the road:

  • Don't start a shift if you're already tired
  • Take a break of at least 15 minutes every two hours
  • Avoid long stints between midnight and 6am, when natural alertness is lowest
  • If you feel sleepy, stop somewhere safe — never on a motorway hard shoulder or in an emergency area

Warning signs to act on

  • Yawning repeatedly, or heavy and stinging eyes
  • Struggling to hold your concentration
  • Reacting late to brake lights, traffic or obstacles
  • Not remembering the last stretch of road you covered
  • Drifting out of your lane, or wobbling and struggling to hold a line
  • Micro-sleeps — eyes closing for a second or two
⚠ Warning

If you notice any of these, stop. Don't try to push on to the drop-off. Winding down a window or turning up the radio does not work — the only things that help are rest and time.

What actually helps

✔ Safety Tip
  • Get a proper night's sleep before a long or late shift
  • If you're caught out, a caffeinated drink followed by a 15–20 minute nap is the most effective short-term fix — caffeine takes about 20 minutes to work, so nap while you wait for it
  • Eat regularly and lightly; heavy meals make drowsiness worse
  • Stay hydrated — dehydration accelerates fatigue
  • Check whether any medication you take causes drowsiness
  • Be honest with yourself about back-to-back shifts. No order is worth a collision.

Support when you need it

If you're too tired to work safely, stop taking orders and go home. Use the in-app support chat if you need to explain a cancelled order. In an emergency, call 999.

Manual Handling — Protecting Your Back

Delivery bags get heavy, and back injuries build up over months rather than happening in one moment. Manual handling is one of the biggest causes of workplace injury in the UK, and the damage is often permanent.

Think before you lift

Under the Manual Handling Operations Regulations 1992, the risk should be assessed before the lift, not after. In practice that means a few seconds of thought:

  • How heavy and how stable is the load?
  • Where is it going, and is the route clear? Steps, kerbs, wet ground?
  • Can you split it into two trips instead of one?

How to lift

✔ Safety Tip
  1. Keep the load close to your waist — the further it is from your body, the more strain goes through your lower back
  2. Get a stable base — feet roughly shoulder-width apart, one slightly forward
  3. Bend your knees, not your back — let your legs do the work
  4. Keep your back straight throughout the lift
  5. Don't twist — move your feet to change direction, keep hips and shoulders in line
  6. Look ahead once you have the load, not down at it
  7. Put it down first, adjust after — don't reposition mid-lift

Know your limits

There is no legal maximum weight in UK law. The HSE publishes guideline figures as a risk filter — roughly 25kg for men and 16kg for women — but these only apply when the load is held close to the body at around waist height. Lifting above shoulder height or down near the floor drops those figures to about 10kg and 7kg. Repetitive lifting, twisting or an awkward grip reduces them further.

If a bag feels too heavy, it is too heavy. Ask the restaurant to split the order, or contact support.

Listen to your body

Aching shoulders or a sore lower back are early warnings, not something to work through. Take a break, and report any injury through the app so it's on record.

Support when you need it

If you injure your back, seek medical attention and report it. For anything urgent, call 999.

E-bike and E-scooter Battery Safety

If you ride an e-bike or electric scooter, this is the most serious risk in this pack — and it isn't on the road. It's where you charge and store the battery.

⚠ Warning

London Fire Brigade attended 206 e-bike and e-scooter fires in 2025 — a record, and an average of around 17 a month. Five people have died in these fires in London since 2023. In every one of those cases, the person who died did not own the e-bike involved. These fires put your family and your neighbours at risk, not just you.

LFB has specifically warned about gig-economy riders using conversion kits that may not meet safety standards.

Why these fires are different

A failing lithium-ion battery can go into thermal runaway. It doesn't smoulder — it can explode, burn at extreme temperatures, spread within seconds and give off toxic gas. It can also reignite hours or even days later. A domestic extinguisher will not put it out.

Buying and converting

  • Buy from a reputable retailer. Batteries and chargers bought through online marketplaces are far less likely to meet UKCA or CE safety standards
  • Never fit a conversion kit to a bike that wasn't designed for one
  • Only ever use the charger supplied for your battery — a mismatched charger is a common cause of failure
  • Never use a damaged or repaired battery, or one from an unknown source

Charging safely

✔ Safety Tip
  • Never charge overnight or while you're asleep
  • Never charge in a hallway, by your front door, or anywhere on your escape route — if it ignites you need to be able to get out
  • Charge somewhere you can see it, away from beds, sofas and anything flammable
  • Unplug once it's fully charged; don't leave it on charge indefinitely
  • Let a battery cool down before charging it after a shift
  • Fit a working smoke alarm in the room where you charge

Stop using it immediately if you notice

  • Swelling, bulging or any change in shape
  • Leaking, or an unusual smell — chemical, sweet or burning
  • Getting unusually hot while charging or riding
  • Cracks, dents or damage after a drop or a crash
  • Discolouration, or making hissing or popping noises

If a battery catches fire

⚠ Warning

Do not attempt to fight it. Get everyone out, close the door behind you, and call 999 from outside. Do not go back in for the bike, your phone or anything else.

Disposal

Never put a lithium-ion battery in your household bin or general recycling — they start fires in bin lorries and waste facilities. Take it to a household waste recycling centre that accepts batteries, and tell staff if it's damaged or swollen.

Support when you need it

For guidance on safe charging, search for the London Fire Brigade #ChargeSafe campaign. In a fire, call 999.

Working in Hot Weather

UK summers are producing more frequent and more intense heat, and couriers are exposed to it for hours at a time. The UK Health Security Agency issues Heat-Health Alerts through the summer — worth checking alongside the weather before a shift.

The risks

  • Dehydration — headache is an early sign; by the time you feel thirsty you're already behind
  • UV exposure — you can burn on an overcast day, and riders are exposed for hours
  • Heat exhaustion — heavy sweating, cramps, dizziness, nausea, extreme tiredness
  • Heatstroke — a medical emergency where the body can no longer regulate its temperature

Heat exhaustion: what to do

Stop delivering. Get into shade or somewhere air-conditioned. Loosen or remove excess clothing. Sip water steadily. Cool your skin with cool water on your face, neck and arms. Rest for at least 30 minutes. If you don't feel better after that, call 111.

Heatstroke: call 999

⚠ Warning

Hot skin that isn't sweating, confusion, a rapid pulse, seizure or loss of consciousness means heatstroke. Call 999 immediately. While you wait, move the person somewhere cool, remove excess clothing and cool their skin with water and fanning. Do not give them anything to drink if they are drowsy or confused.

Preventing it

✔ Safety Tip
  • Carry water and refill whenever you can — don't wait until you're thirsty
  • Wear loose, light-coloured, breathable layers; long sleeves protect from UV better than bare skin
  • Use SPF 30+ and reapply every few hours — don't miss ears, neck and the backs of your hands
  • Take breaks in shade rather than pushing through
  • Check whether your medication increases heat sensitivity — ask a pharmacist
  • Remember your thermal bag insulates in both directions — food still needs to move quickly in heat

Support when you need it

If conditions feel unsafe, stop and use the in-app support chat. For heat exhaustion that isn't improving, call 111. For heatstroke, call 999.
